**Last Updated:** 2026-06-07

## Stage 0 — Early Pattern Recognition (Elementary–Middle School)

### Multiplication Table (Elementary School)
*First experience of mathematical structure as pattern*

The multiplication table was the earliest encounter with the idea that mathematical objects have internal structure. Not isolated facts, but a grid where:
- Each row/column has a consistent pattern
- Tables interact with each other (e.g., 6×7 = 7×6)
- Structure can be remembered through pattern, not memorization

**Retrospective connection:** This is possibly the earliest instance of H2 (Representation Transformation) — the grid format made structure visible that the individual equations do not.

---

### Algebraic Identities (Middle School)
*Structure discoverable through geometric representation*

Algebraic expansions like (a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b² are not merely symbolic manipulation — they correspond to rectangular areas. The symbol and the shape are the same structure in different representations.

**Retrospective connection:** H2 again — a non-obvious representation (geometric area) reveals structure hidden in the symbolic form.

---

### Euler Characteristic (Middle School)
*First encounter with invariance*

V − E + F = 2 holds for all convex polyhedra regardless of how different they look. The same number survives across wildly different shapes.

**Retrospective connection:** First encounter with structural invariance — the idea that something can be preserved under transformation. This would later become central to Papers 1–3 and to H4.

---

## Stage 1 — Pre-University Observations (High School)

### Korean CSAT Nonfiction Reading
*Structure as a reading strategy*

Complex academic texts can be navigated by understanding their logical architecture rather than memorizing every detail. Structure enables efficient access to information.

**Retrospective connection:** Early intuition that structure and content are distinct — understanding the former enables mastery of the latter.

---

### Function Construction (Mathematics)
*Structure as synthesis*

Multiple constraints can be integrated into a single coherent function. Mathematical creativity often involves finding the structural form that satisfies all conditions simultaneously.

**Retrospective connection:** Research question generation may work similarly — multiple observations synthesize into a single structural explanation.

---

### Genetics and Pascal's Triangle
*Same structure, different phenomena*

Genetic inheritance ratios (dominant/recessive offspring probabilities) match Pascal's Triangle entries. Two completely unrelated domains share the same underlying combinatorial structure.

**Retrospective connection:** This may have been the first observation that the same structure can "exist" in multiple domains — a precursor to H2 and H4 (generalization as significance criterion).

---

### Symmetric Boolean Functions (High School)
*First encounter with the phenomena that would become Paper 2*

Certain Boolean functions remain unchanged when variable labels are exchanged. Symmetry as a structural property of Boolean functions was first noticed here.

**Research outcome:** Became the direct starting point for Paper 2 (Symmetric Boolean Function Minor Thesis).

---

## Stage 2 — University: First Research Phase (Boolean Structures)

### Karnaugh Maps — Visual Pattern Discovery
*Starting point of the formal research program*

Boolean functions visualized on Karnaugh maps reveal large-scale patterns immediately:
- Checkerboards for XOR/XNOR
- Ring, corner, point patterns for symmetric functions
- Pattern visibility that exceeds what the Boolean expression reveals

**Key observation:** Pattern recognition preceded computation. The structure was seen before it was calculated.

**Research outcome:** Paper 1 (1KMapStructureInvariance)

**SRT connection:** H2 (representation reveals structure), H5 (explanation anticipated before found)

---

### Hamming Weight Layer Structure
*Conceptual breakthrough for Paper 2*

The recognition that symmetric Boolean functions select specific Hamming Weight Layers explained why they produce particular visual patterns. The layers are the underlying structure; the patterns are the visible manifestation.

**Key observation:** The pattern was perceived visually before the layer structure was identified analytically. Structure recognition preceded structural explanation.

**Research outcome:** Paper 2 (2SymmetricBooleanFunctionMinorThesis)

**SRT connection:** H1 (humans recognized the structure before computing it), H2, H5

---

### Variable Rearrangement Invariance
*The invariance question becomes explicit*

When input variables of a Boolean function are rearranged, the visual pattern on the Karnaugh map changes dramatically — but the logical function is preserved. The research question became: what structural properties survive rearrangement?

**Key observation:** The research focus was on *what is preserved*, not *what changed*. Invariance passed the significance filter; change did not.

**Research outcome:** Paper 3 (3VariableRearrangementInvarianceMinorThesis)

**SRT connection:** H4 (invariance as significance criterion), H6 (explanatory significance of invariance)

---

## Stage 3 — Meta-Theoretical Turn (Structure Recognition Theory)

### The Meta-Question Emerges
*When the question becomes about questions*

After Papers 1–3, a new kind of question emerged: not "what is the structure?" but "why did humans find these structures interesting?" The research program turned inward on itself.

This is the transition from:
- Studying Boolean structures (Papers 1–3)
to:
- Studying why humans study structures (Paper 4)

**Key insight:** The sequence of discoveries in Papers 1–3 is itself data about how research questions are generated.

---

### Research Generation Framework Formulated
*The Pattern → Research progression becomes explicit*

The observed sequence:
1. Pattern noticed (KMap checkerboard)
2. Pattern felt non-random ("this should have an explanation")
3. Structure sought and found (XOR)
4. Research question generalized

This sequence was formalized as: Pattern → Attention → Structure → Meaning → Question → Research

**Research outcome:** Core framework of Paper 4 (4StructureRecognitionTheory)

---

### Hypotheses H1–H7 Developed
*Exploratory theoretical framework*

Seven exploratory hypotheses were developed to explain different aspects of the research generation process:

| Hypothesis | Core Question |
|---|---|
| H1: Structure Discoverer | Do humans have a structural recognition advantage? |
| H2: Representation Transformation | Can representation changes reveal invisible structures? |
| H3: Attention Filter | Why do only some patterns receive attention? |
| H4: Significance Filter | Why do only some structures generate research? |
| H5: Explanation Anticipation | Do humans anticipate explanations before finding them? |
| H6: Explanatory Significance | Does research begin from detecting explanatory potential? |
| H7: Mature Structure Discoverer | Is the core human role evaluating explanatory significance? |

## Stage 4 — Methodological Self-Reflection (Human-AI Collaboration)

### The Research Process Becomes the Research Object
*Paper 5 — studying the process that made Papers 1–4 possible*

Long-term collaboration between a human researcher and multiple AI systems (ChatGPT, Claude, ANTIGRAVITY) generated observations not just about Boolean structures, but about the collaboration process itself:

- Context loss occurs across sessions, AI systems, and time
- Human researchers preserve goals while AI systems reconstruct context
- Documentation quality is an economic variable
- Future versions of the researcher are active collaborators

These observations generated Human-AI Research Continuity Theory (HARCT).

**Research outcome:** Paper 5 (5HumanAIResearchCollaboration) — COMPLETE (2026-06-06)

---

### HARCT Framework (2026)
*Human-AI Research Continuity Theory*

The central claim:

> Long-term Human-AI research collaboration is fundamentally a problem of research continuity rather than intelligence alone.

Four theoretical components:
1. Goal Preservation Theory
2. Cognitive Architecture (three-component model)
3. Reconstruction Cost Theory
4. Externalized Memory

**Connection to SRT:** HARCT is consistent with H7 — humans preserve goals (evaluating what is worth pursuing) while AI systems reconstruct context (processing and connecting within established directions).

### Program-Level Central Question Formalized (2026-06-06)
*The research program's framing question raised to its highest level*

The question **"How do humans come to see something new?"** was formally adopted as the program-level central question, superseding the theory-level question as the program's broadest framing.

| Level | Question | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Program | How do humans come to see something new? | Entire research program |
| Theory | How do humans detect explanatory significance? | Paper 4 operational focus |

The program-level question spans all eight levels of the CoreQuestions.md hierarchy:
- Level 0–5: What happens in the encounter with a phenomenon (observation → explanation)
- Level 6–8: What happens as concepts change what can be encountered (concept → expanded perception → new discovery)

Together, the two levels of the question describe the full cycle: the encounter with structure (Levels 0–5) and the conceptual evolution that determines what encounters are possible (Levels 6–8).
